04 THE DATA ROOM

Tiny model.
Real limits.

The probability comes from records, not a dial.
Open the hood. Keep your skepticism.

What is a “hit” in this game?

The game asks whether reported worldwide theatrical gross reached 2.5× reported production budget. This is an adjustable comparison rule, not an industry definition of profit or break-even. Theater shares, marketing, financing, taxes, and later revenue are absent. The backlot lets you try 1× and 5× too.

Where does the data come from—and what was removed?

We use the financial columns of FiveThirtyEight’s publicly released Bechdel dataset, originally assembled for a different research question. It is a selected historical sample, not a census of films. Bechdel labels are not model inputs. Of 1,794 records, 51 shared ambiguous financial signatures and 17 lacked usable financial fields. We exclude these 68 rows instead of silently correcting them.

The source labels one field intgross; its values for cross-checked films are worldwide totals. We use it as reported worldwide gross and do not add domestic gross again. The downloaded snapshot, checksums, and exclusion report are preserved. Other errors can remain.

How is the probability calculated?

A regularized logistic regression uses log production budget and release year. Training uses 1,188 films from 1970–2008; evaluation uses 538 different films from 2009–2013. Neither later gross nor ratings, awards, posters, or executive identity enters the model. A constant training-set base rate and a smoothed budget-band estimate provide comparisons.

This is a retrospective backtest using an archived dataset. Budgets and historical grosses were collected after release, so it is not proof of what a forecaster could have known on an actual greenlight date. It is not a modern release predictor or a measure of executive skill.

What do my points mean?

Each forecast earns 100 × [1 − (probability − outcome)²] points. Outcome is 1 when the film clears the mark, 0 otherwise. The five-film maximum is 500. This rewards useful probabilities and makes confident misses costly. Five curated, recognizable films cannot establish your general forecasting ability; memory of an outcome can help. The full 538-film evaluation is reported separately.

The poster-blind option invites a comparison of judgments. It does not randomly assign participants or establish that images cause a change. Best scores are saved only in this browser when storage is available; nothing is uploaded.

How does this fulfill COMM 502’s design brief?

Engage: a familiar movie, a concrete decision, and an achievable five-round loop invite participation. Interact: one decision at a time, an optional evidence reveal, and a visible progress strip manage attention and control. Understand: the original forecast stays next to the outcome; budgets and grosses get different labels; executive timelines separate attribution from timing.

These choices explore framing, prior knowledge, representativeness, motivation, and attribution. They are design hypotheses to connect to the assigned course readings and evaluate with users—not findings that the prototype has already proved.
