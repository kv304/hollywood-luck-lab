What supports the numbers?

A compact account of the data, evaluation, and design choices. The complete source audit and model report are linked below.

The question and the target

The lab explores how people judge film performance, what evidence they inspect, and how framing a result changes its apparent meaning. The game’s default event is reported worldwide theatrical gross reaching 2.5 times reported production budget. This is a comparison rule, not profit, return on investment, artistic quality, or an industry definition of a hit.

Two film sources, distinct roles

The forecasting targets come from FiveThirtyEight’s Bechdel financial dataset. Of 1,794 records, 51 with ambiguous repeated financial signatures and 17 without usable finances are excluded. That leaves 1,726 films: 1,188 through 2008 for training, and 538 from 2009–2013 for evaluation. The source’s intgross is treated as the reported worldwide total; domestic gross is not added again.

The TMDB 5000 publisher snapshot supplies cast, director, genre, company, and release metadata. Unique normalized title and exact year match 1,564 original records, including 496 evaluation films. The broader career explorer uses the TMDB snapshot’s own financial fields; these can differ from the game’s figures. Its 2,671 profiles cover selected directors and the first eight billed cast members, not complete careers. Missing budgets and grosses are omitted from financial aggregates rather than treated as zero.

Model selection and leakage boundaries

Six predefined candidates are compared: two regularized logistic models, two random forests, and two boosted-tree models. A fit through 2004 is compared on 2005–2008; lowest validation Brier error selects the model. It is refit through 2008 and evaluated on 2009–2013. Historical career features exclude a film’s whole release year and freeze at the relevant training cutoff.

Inputs include budget, year, genre, release-month terms, language, metadata availability, and smoothed earlier-film performance and coverage for credited directors, leading cast, and production companies. Later gross, ratings, reviews, popularity, future credits, and executive identity are excluded. The three target probabilities receive a prediction-only monotonic adjustment so a harder gross threshold cannot become more likely than an easier one.

The observed gain is modest. A 2,000-resample paired bootstrap interval for the forecast-score gain includes no improvement. Resampling treats films as independent, an imperfect assumption for franchises and shared teams. Some probability bands are poorly calibrated. This evaluation sample was already examined during development of the older version; it is not a pristine external benchmark.

What an explanation means

A feature-sensitivity check replaces one input with its training median and recalculates the estimate. The differences are not additive and do not identify causal drivers of box-office success. Earlier comparable films are selected by budget and genre similarity without selecting on their outcomes. The probability-range check compares average forecasts with observed outcomes in the same historical evaluation sample; it is not an independent validation or a per-film confidence interval.

Forecast points equal 100 × [1 − (probability − outcome)²] per film. One realized outcome cannot establish that a percentage was well calibrated. A familiar five-film deck, prior knowledge, repeated play, and optional model help cannot establish general forecasting skill.

Communication design is a hypothesis to test

One decision at a time and visible progress manage attention. Forecasts remain beside outcomes to make hindsight easier to notice. Switching gross and gross/budget exposes framing choices; executive year chapters put attribution in temporal context. Drawing five different observed films illustrates selective evidence. These design hypotheses connect to COMM 502’s emphasis on engagement, interaction, and understanding. No randomized experiment or user study has yet demonstrated learning, engagement, or causal effects.

Reproducibility, authorship, and rights

Karan Verma defined the project direction and iterated the use cases and interface, with AI assistance for research, implementation, debugging, and test development. The repository includes rebuild scripts, pinned modeling dependencies, source checksums, joins, and automated checks. The interface runs as static HTML/CSS/JavaScript, with no account or response-upload backend.

FiveThirtyEight’s data is CC BY 4.0. The TMDB publisher snapshot is labeled “Other (specified in description)” and refers to TMDB terms; it is not CC0. Artwork and portraits retain their separate source rights and credits. This product uses the TMDB API-derived dataset but is not endorsed or certified by TMDB. Historical budgets, metadata, and grosses were assembled retrospectively; final credits and release timing may not have been known at greenlight.
